# Phase 0 Experiment — does the "same items, new lens" twist delight a child?

> The cheapest test of the only bet that gates everything. **No code. ~10 minutes.
> Paper cards you already own.** Run it before building anything.

---

## The materials (all images already in `Assets/`)

Print these 8 cards (one picture each, big, no labels needed):

| Animal | File | Lives | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lion | `lion_1.png` | land | alive |
| Elephant | `elephant_1.png` | land | alive |
| T-Rex | `trex_1.png` | land | extinct |
| Triceratops | `triceratops_1.png` | land | extinct |
| Shark | `shark_1.png` | water | alive |
| Tuna | `tuna_1.png` | water | alive |
| Mosasaurus | `mosasaurus_1.png` | water | extinct |
| Plesiosaurus | `plesiosaurus_1.png` | water | extinct |

These 8 are chosen so they split **cleanly two different ways** — and the two ways
*cross-cut*, which is what creates the surprise.

---

## The script

**Round 1 — first lens (habitat).**
Lay all 8 cards out, shuffled. Say:
> "Put these into two groups for me — the ones that live on **land**, and the ones that
> live in **water**."

Expected clean answer:
- Land: Lion, Elephant, T-Rex, Triceratops
- Water: Shark, Tuna, Mosasaurus, Plesiosaurus

**Round 2 — the magic (new lens, same animals).**
Keep the *same 8 cards*. Say:
> "Now… the **same** animals. This time, two groups: the ones that are **still alive
> today**, and the ones that are **extinct** — gone forever."

Expected clean answer:
- Alive: Lion, Elephant, **Shark, Tuna**
- Extinct: T-Rex, Triceratops, **Mosasaurus, Plesiosaurus**

**Watch the micro-moment.** When the shark *leaves the sea-monster group to stand next to
the lion* — that flicker on the child's face is the entire experiment. ("Wait, the shark
goes with the *lion* now?!")

**Round 3 — optional, only if they're enjoying it.**
> "Is there *another* way you could split them?"
See if they invent a lens themselves (big/small, scary/friendly, eats-meat/eats-plants).
A child inventing a new dimension unprompted is the strongest possible yes.

> **Why naming the lens matters:** these 8 group cleanly *both* ways, so a silent puzzle
> would be ambiguous (that's the §6 fairness problem). Saying the lens out loud each round
> removes the ambiguity — and naming it is exactly the right move for a facilitated test.

---

## Decide the verdict BEFORE you start (don't move the goalposts after)

**A YES (delight) looks like:**
- A spontaneous "ohh!" / surprise at Round 2's regroup.
- Re-sorts willingly; holds that one animal belongs to two different groups.
- Asks "can we do another one?" or invents a third lens unprompted.
- Leans in, wants to keep going.

**A NO (confusion / flat) looks like:**
- Frustration that "the answer changed"; wants the one "right" answer.
- Sorts randomly, gives up, or asks to stop.
- Completes it but is indifferent — neither lit up nor bothered (treat as a soft no).

**Write down which you saw, in one line, right after.** If it's a clear yes → build the
software Phase 0 in `Ishans_games_4`. If no → we iterate the rounds/age before any code.

---

## Notes
- **Try a 9–10-year-old first.** If the regroup lands there, then try younger — the answer
  may be age-dependent, and that's useful data, not failure.
- **Run it 2–3 times** (different kids if you can). One polite kid isn't signal.
- Keep it light and playful — it's a game, not a test. *You* should be having fun too.
